Human Anatomy Explorer

I tore a tendon in my shoulder and realised I had no mental image of the muscle involved. So I built the thing that would have shown me it.

Open the explorer

Problem

The supraspinatus runs under a bony shelf. Nothing photographs it there, and a flat diagram gives you an outline rather than a shape you can hold in your head. I had just torn a tendon in mine and could not picture the muscle it belonged to.

Paid anatomy apps cover this ground. I built my own.

Approach

The interesting problem turned out not to be rendering. My first version described each muscle as two points and a radius, and it looked like a stick figure. No amount of lighting was going to fix that, because the shapes carried no anatomical information for the lighting to reveal.

That reframed the work. The rebuild generates every muscle at load time, either by sweeping a cross-section along a curved spline, or by lofting a sheet between a broad origin and a narrow insertion. The geometry became the thing that carries the anatomy, rather than a container for it.

What I built

  • 191 muscles, a skeleton and a face, all generated at load time rather than shipped as model files.
  • Two generation strategies: a cross-section swept along a curved spline for muscles that run in a line, and a sheet lofted from a broad origin to a narrow insertion for the ones that fan.
  • Ambient occlusion baked into the vertices, so the depth cue costs nothing at runtime and phones get it for free.
  • 34 automated browser tests covering it.

What it demonstrates

  • Finding the actual problem, not the obvious one Everything about the first version said "this needs to look better", which points you at lighting and materials. The real fault was upstream, in a data model too crude to describe the thing it was modelling. That is the same mistake as shipping a redesign when the underlying model is wrong.
  • Generated geometry instead of shipped assets 191 muscles built at load time from a description, rather than authored and downloaded as files.
  • A performance decision made once, at build time Baking occlusion into the vertices moves the cost off the phone entirely, which is what makes the depth read on a mid-range device.
  • Tested, for a personal project 34 automated browser tests on something nobody asked me to ship.

Tech used

  • three.js
  • Vanilla ES modules
  • No build step
  • Procedural geometry
  • Baked ambient occlusion
  • Automated browser tests

01What I help with

The unglamorous parts, done properly

Most people don't need a clever fund. They need a goal written down, an amount that actually fits their income, and someone who checks in when life changes.

Planning

  • Goal mapping — putting a number and a date on what you're saving for, before picking anything.
  • Right-sizing the amount so the SIP survives a bad month rather than being abandoned in one.
  • Asset allocation matched to when you need the money, not to what performed well last year.
  • Emergency fund first — the boring precondition that keeps you from redeeming at the worst time.

Setting up & keeping it running

  • KYC and onboarding handled end to end, so the paperwork isn't the reason you never start.
  • SIP setup and automation — mandates, dates, step-ups as your income grows.
  • Scheduled reviews rather than reacting to headlines: is this still on track for the goal?
  • Rebalancing and switches when allocation drifts, with the tax consequence explained first.
  • Consolidating existing folios so you can see everything in one place.

02How it works

Four steps, no jargon

  1. 01

    A conversation

    What you earn, what you owe, what you're saving for and by when. No products discussed. If a mutual fund isn't the right answer, I'll say so.

  2. 02

    A written plan

    Goals, timelines, how much per month, and where it goes — in plain language, on one page, so you can question any part of it.

  3. 03

    Setting it up

    KYC, folios, mandates and SIP dates. You approve every transaction yourself; I never hold your money or operate your account.

  4. 04

    Reviews on a schedule

    A periodic check on whether you're still on track, plus a call whenever something real changes — a raise, a move, a new goal.

05FAQ

The questions worth asking

What does this cost me?

You don't pay me a fee. As a distributor I'm paid a commission by the asset management company on investments made through me, and that cost already sits inside the scheme's expense ratio for regular plans.

It's not free money — a regular plan carries a higher expense ratio than a direct plan. That's the honest trade: you're paying for someone to set it up, review it and answer the phone. If you'd rather do all of that yourself, direct plans are cheaper and I'll tell you so.

Are you an investment adviser?

No. I'm a mutual fund distributor, not a SEBI-registered investment adviser (RIA). The difference matters: an RIA charges you a fee and can advise across products; a distributor helps you invest in mutual funds and is paid by the AMC.

I'll help you decide what fits your goals, but for anything outside mutual funds — or if you want fully fee-only advice — you want an RIA, and I'll say so rather than stretch.

Can you guarantee returns?

No, and nobody honestly can. Mutual funds carry market risk, and the value of your investment can fall as well as rise. Past performance tells you what happened, not what will.

What I can do is match the risk to your timeline, so a bad year doesn't arrive at the exact moment you need the money.

Do you hold my money?

Never. Your money goes directly from your bank account to the AMC, and the units are held in your own folio in your name. I facilitate and service the investment — I don't take custody of it, and I can't transact without your approval.

What if I already invest somewhere?

That's common and completely fine. We can consolidate existing folios so everything is visible in one place, review what you're holding and why, and leave alone anything that's working. Switching for the sake of switching has a tax cost, and I'd rather not hand you one.

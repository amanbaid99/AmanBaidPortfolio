Teardowns

Product Teardowns & Feature Proposals

Nobody briefed these. I wrote them because the problem was interesting, which makes them the clearest record of how I think when there is no backlog telling me what to look at. Each one separates what I observed from what I would change and what would still need testing. Full write-ups on Notion.

Zomato & Blinkit · Feature proposal

Selective item ordering, inside the cart

Observed

When the total goes over budget the choice is binary: order everything, or delete something and lose it. Deleting means leaving the cart to find the item again, and on a grocery order it means tapping the minus button fifteen times.

The cart is the decision-making page. Making someone leave it to change their mind is what turns a hesitation into an abandonment.

I proposed

A checkbox per line: uncheck to leave an item out of this order without losing it. The write-up spends most of its length on what that breaks: coupons that lapse when the subtotal drops under the threshold, free-delivery minimums, combo pricing, and the microcopy that has to explain each one.

Not validated

The impact is a hypothesis, not a result. The write-up states the A/B test that would settle it, and frames the revenue as incremental order value against take rate rather than the headline cart total. Nothing here was shipped or measured.

Scapia · Teardown

A credit card as a behaviour engine

Observed

Rather than ask whether the card is good value, I asked what behaviour it is built to produce. Spend ₹10,000 this month and the benefits unlock next month, which rewards consistency rather than a single splurge. Unlimited lounge access is not a perk so much as a filter that only frequent flyers can use. Airport spending gets reframed from a place you avoid into a place that pays you.

The coins look like cashback and are not. They redeem only against travel booked inside the app, and the balance goes on the same card. Controlled generosity, not generosity.

No proposal

This one is a teardown, not a feature proposal. It is a record of reading someone else’s incentive design closely, and I am not claiming a change to it.
